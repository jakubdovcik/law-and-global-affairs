# Lecture 10: State responsibility and countermeasures

## what Kennedy stressed

- Territorial doctrines work like **jurisdictional bases**: claims to be asserted, weighed and contested.
- **State responsibility is the hinge** between the bucket of norms and the bucket of remedies. It matters precisely because nothing robust exists instead: norms don't specify penalties, and there is no standing criminal enforcement or compulsory court. It 'legalizes the interactive enforcement process' ([[ilc-arsiwa]]).
- **Proportionality is 'not a calculator'.** It weighs the norm breached against the norm the countermeasure violates. In [[gabcikovo]] riparian rights weighed heavily. In [[air-services]] France held out despite a $100 vs $1,000 asymmetry, so principle mattered more than cost: account for what the other side *values*.
- **State responsibility legalises the ratchet** (retorsion → countermeasures); proportionality mirrors military deterrence. The prior wrong is always contested, and Air Services lets 'in one state's view' be enough.
- **Repurposing** ([[lillich-paxman]]): with no agreed definition of terrorism, a due-diligence duty to suppress non-state actors becomes a sovereignty failure that justifies intervention. The environment could follow (emissions as breach, so extensive countermeasures).
- **Gabčíkovo affirmed both sides' principles** and resolved nothing. The Court creates a *legal plane* on which the dispute continues: law's **translation** function. The Secretary-General's two hats: adjudication (rights) vs mediation (interests).
- Next in the course: history of international legal thought, then substantive commitments (human rights, environment) and critical reinterpretations from about 2000.

## my notes

### Territorial Doctrine: Modes and Tensions

- Territorial acquisition rests on multiple competing bases: discovery, conquest, intensified sovereignty, uti possidetis
- Uti possidetis: pre-independence (often colonial) boundaries take priority in postcolonial disputes
- Frequently overrides factual control arguments
- Can be interrupted by self-determination claims
- Doctrines function less like rules and more like jurisdiction bases: claims to be asserted, weighed, and contested
- Western Sahara: replaced sovereignty/title with “legal ties,” recognizing non-Eurocentric modes of political reality
- Nomadic populations studied sociologically to determine ties without requiring sovereignty
- Decolonization moment: self-determination ultimately supersedes prior ties, even significant ones

### Internationalization: Three Modes (Berman)

- Formalism: adjudicate based on who holds what rights (weighing and balancing)
- Functionalism: focus on interests, not rights; negotiate a pragmatic solution
- Cultural/symbolic: contending parties hold deep value-based claims (e.g., Jerusalem’s religious significance)
- Connects to human rights and environmental protection as the course moves forward
- These modes map onto a recurring tension: rights-based adjudication vs. interest-based problem-solving

### State Responsibility: Structure and Purpose

- Core rule: a state that breaches an international obligation incurs responsibility; injury triggers a duty of reparation
- State responsibility sits between the body of norms and the available remedies
- Norms don’t self-enforce; no supreme sovereign to legislate or enforce
- State responsibility legalizes the interactive enforcement process
- Functions as a “hinge”: connects the norm bucket to the remedy bucket
- Arises to significance precisely because robust alternatives don’t exist:
- Norms don’t specify their own penalties
- No standing international criminal enforcement
- No universal compulsory court jurisdiction

### Countermeasures: Justification and Limits

- Countermeasures: otherwise-illegal acts justified by a prior breach
- Procedural limits: must negotiate before acting; can’t act while meaningful arbitration is underway
- Substantive limit: proportionality, calibrated to injury suffered and importance of the rights/norms involved
- Proportionality is not a calculator: factors include the weight of the norm breached and the norm violated by the countermeasure
- Gabčíkovo-Nagymaros (Slovakia/Hungary): Czech Republic overdid its response by taking full dam control
- Court weighed riparian rights as especially significant
- Case still live: recent ministerial deal to supply power to Hungary at cost price
- Pavel Tomka built his ICJ career on this case
- Air Services (US/France): France refused to concede despite $100 vs. $1,000 economic asymmetry
- Suggests a matter of principle (sovereignty, treaty interpretation) outweighed economic cost
- Illustrates how proportionality must account for what the other party actually values

### The Enforcement Problem: Horizontal Society

- No hierarchy enforces norms; enforcement is interactive and decentralized
- A norm becomes effective through persuasion, pressure, or both
- Good argument + political/military power are hard to disentangle
- State responsibility legalizes the ratchet: each escalatory step is framed as a lawful countermeasure
- Retorsion (legal acts: withdrawing ambassador, sanctions) → countermeasures (otherwise-illegal acts)
- Proportionality doctrine mirrors deterrence logic in military strategy
- The prior wrongful act requirement is always contested: the acting state claims breach; the other denies it
- No adjudicative machinery to resolve this in most cases
- “In one state’s view” standard (Air Services): subjective belief of breach is sufficient to enter countermeasures land

### State Responsibility as Substantive Norm-Creation

- State responsibility can be repurposed to create substantive obligations where treaty consensus is impossible
- Terrorism example: no global treaty definition possible (“one person’s terrorist is another’s freedom fighter”)
- State responsibility reframed as a due-diligence obligation to suppress non-state actors on one’s territory
- Failure to suppress = sovereignty failure = justification for intervention
- Environmental law parallel: allowing deforestation/emissions that harm other states could constitute a breach
- Principle at stake (global warming) is large, so proportional countermeasures could be extensive
- Intervention and state responsibility become entangled: failed-state logic enables sovereignty override

### Gabčíkovo and the 1,000-Year Dispute

- The ‘97 ruling affirmed both parties’ principles without resolving the underlying dispute
- Slovakia read it as a win: the 1977 treaty was held still in force
- Hungary’s breach acknowledged; Slovakia’s countermeasure found disproportionate
- Court’s role: create a legal plane within which the dispute can continue, not terminate it
- “Translation” function of law: converts a water/territory/historical conflict into a debate about breach and norm hierarchy
- Secretary General analogy: adjudicative hat (rights) vs. mediation hat (interests)
